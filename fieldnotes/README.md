ur examples make this much more interesting than a generic paddock planner:
“Can I put a 50-foot greenhouse here, connect it to the existing lane, and still get equipment past it?”

Or:
“Where should a handling area go so sheep can reach it from several fields, and how does that work with the slope?”

The product could let someone:
- Map existing lanes, fences, gates, buildings, and water.
- Place correctly scaled greenhouse, barn, and handling-area footprints.
- Rotate and adjust those layouts.
- Draw proposed access routes and inspect their elevation profiles.
- Compare alternatives and export an annotated plan with measurements.
The distinctive feature would be helping people understand the consequences of placement. A building fits on a map, but what is the slope across its footprint? A lane connects two places, but how steep is the route? A corral occupies enough area, but where do animals enter and where does a trailer approach?
